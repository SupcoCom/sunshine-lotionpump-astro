Each is preventable with the right specification—not with “better quality control” after the fact.</p><p><strong>1. Leakage at the neck during use.</strong> Root cause: neck finish mismatch between bottle and sprayer. A 28/410 sprayer on a bottle with a slightly out-of-tolerance neck thread will leak when inverted or squeezed. Prevention: Specify both sprayer and bottle from the same supplier, or provide the supplier with a bottle sample so thread engagement can be verified on the actual bottle tooling. Tighter thread tolerance is within +/- 0.1mm—anything looser risks intermittent sealing.</p><p><strong>2. Trigger mechanism fatigue (reduced spray output after repeated use).</strong> Root cause: The plastic spring element in the trigger loses elasticity after 500–1,000 cycles in cheap designs. Prevention: Request a trigger sprayer with a metal return spring if the application requires sustained use (car wash, garden). For standard household cleaners, PP/PE blended trigger mechanisms (like WP-1239) extend cycle life. Ask the factory for cycle-test data—a production-grade trigger sprayer should maintain ≥95% output consistency at 2,000 cycles.</p><p><strong>3. Clogged nozzle (inconsistent or blocked spray).</strong> Root cause: Insufficient filtration of the dip tube inlet, or nozzle orifice too small for the liquid viscosity. Prevention: If your product contains particulates or has viscosity above water at 20°C, specify the viscosity (in centipoise) and particulate size (in microns) in the RFQ so the nozzle orifice and dip tube filter screen can be sized accordingly.</p><p><strong>4. Chemical degradation (brittle housing, swollen gasket).</strong> Root cause: PP housing exposed to solvent-based or strongly oxidative chemicals without material verification. Standard PP tolerates most household formulations, but bleach (sodium hypochlorite >5%), hydrogen peroxide >3%, and solvent-based degreasers require verified gasket compatibility. Prevention: Provide the full ingredient list or SDS to the sprayer manufacturer before confirming the SKU. Request chemical immersion test results for the specific formulation.</p><p><strong>5. Dip tube length mismatch.</strong> Root cause: Standard dip tubes are cut to a generic length; if your bottle is taller or shorter than the assumed standard (typically 200–300ml bottle profile), the tube either won’t reach the bottom (leaving product unusable) or curls at the bottom (drawing air). Prevention: Provide bottle height and diameter to the sprayer supplier. Dip tube length can be adjusted during production with no tooling change—it’s a cut-length specification, not a mold variable.</p><h2>Pricing, MOQ, and What Drives Cost Per Unit</h2><p>Trigger sprayer unit pricing is driven by six variables, ranked by impact <!-- kb:kb_product_params (moq, lead_time_days, monthly_capacity, material, standards) -->:</p><table><thead><tr><th>Cost Driver</th><th>Impact on Unit Price</th><th>Notes</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td><strong>Order Volume</strong></td><td>Highest</td><td>10K vs. 100K units: roughly 25–40% unit price reduction at scale.
