<h2>Introduction: Why “24/410” Is the Most Important Number on Your Bottle Specification</h2><p>If you have ever ordered bottle caps and found they do not fit your bottles, you already understand why neck finish dimensions are non-negotiable. The numbers “20/410,” “24/410,” and “28/410” are not arbitrary — they define a precise mechanical interface between your bottle and closure. A 24/410 cap will NOT thread onto a 28/410 bottle, and forcing it creates stress cracks, leaks, and customer returns. Yet many first-time buyers select a cap size based on “what looks right” rather than what their bottle neck actually measures. This guide explains exactly what these numbers mean, which size fits which application, and how to verify compatibility before you order 10,000+ units.</p><h2>Decoding the Numbers: What “24/410” Actually Means</h2><p>The neck finish designation follows the <strong>Glass Packaging Institute (GPI) standard</strong>, now universally adopted for plastic bottles as well. The format is: <strong>[outer diameter in mm] / [thread finish code]</strong>.</p><ul><li><strong>The first number (20, 24, 28):</strong> The outer diameter of the bottle neck opening in millimeters. A 24/410 cap fits a bottle whose neck outer diameter is approximately 24mm.</li><li><strong>The second number (410, 412):</strong> The thread finish code. “410” indicates a single-start thread with 1.5 turns to close; “412” indicates a taller thread profile but also with a single-start configuration. The 410 finish is by far the most common in cosmetic and personal care packaging.</li></ul><p>Critical tolerance: the neck outer diameter tolerance is typically ±0.3mm. A cap designed for 24.0mm nominal may not seal reliably on a 24.5mm neck — this is why <strong>cap and bottle should always be sourced as a matched pair</strong> or at minimum verified on the same physical sample.</p><p><!-- kb:v_product_full size data: 20/410, 24/410, 28/410, 28/412 --></p><h2>Disc Top Cap Size Specifications: The Complete Table</h2><figure class="wp-block-image size-large diagram"><a href="/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2026/05/neck-size-comparison-1.png" target="_blank" rel="noopener"><img decoding="async" src="/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2026/05/neck-size-comparison-1.png" alt="Disc Top Cap Neck Sizes — GPI Standard Finish Dimensions and Measurement Guide" style="max-width:100%;height:auto;display:block" /></a><figcaption>Fig 1: Disc Top Cap Neck Sizes — GPI Standard Finish Dimensions and Measurement Guide</figcaption></figure><table><thead><tr><th>Neck Size</th><th>Outer Diameter (mm)</th><th>Thread Finish</th><th>Common Applications</th><th>Typical Bottle Volume</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td><strong>20/410</strong></td><td>20.0 ±0.3</td><td>Single-start, 1.5 turns</td><td>Serums, treatment products, travel sizes, essential oils</td><td>15ml–50ml</td></tr><tr><td><strong>24/410</strong></td><td>24.0 ±0.3</td><td>Single-start, 1.5 turns</td><td>Shampoo, conditioner, body wash, hand soap, lotion</td><td>50ml–500ml</td></tr><tr><td><strong>28/410</strong></td><td>28.0 ±0.3</td><td>Single-start, 1.5 turns</td><td>Family-size shampoo, body wash, cleaning products</td><td>250ml–1000ml</td></tr><tr><td><strong>28/412</strong></td><td>28.0 ±0.3</td><td>Single-start, taller profile</td><td>Specialty bottles requiring deeper thread engagement</td><td>250ml–1000ml</td></tr></tbody></table><p><!-- kb:v_product_full.size_min, size_max data from actual product catalog --></p><p>At Sunshine Plastic, we stock disc top cap tooling for all four sizes: <strong>20/410, 24/410, 28/410, and 28/412</strong>. It is not. Here is what actually happens when you mix sizes:</p><ul><li><strong>24/410 cap on a 20/410 bottle:</strong> Cap is too loose; threads skip; no seal forms.</li><li><strong>20/410 cap on a 24/410 bottle:</strong> Cap cannot thread on at all — the inner diameter is too small to engage the bottle threads.</li><li><strong>24/410 cap on a 28/410 bottle:</strong> Cap sits on top of the neck but threads won’t engage; leaks immediately on inversion.</li><li><strong>28/410 cap on a 24/410 bottle:</strong> Cap is visibly oversized; partial thread engagement at best; cross-threading is inevitable.</li></ul><p>The only exception: <strong>28/410 and 28/412 caps can sometimes interchange</strong> because the outer diameter is the same (28mm) — the difference is thread height. A 28/410 cap may partially engage on a 28/412 bottle (short engagement) and vice versa (tight fit). Always verify physically before committing.</p><h2>Material and Finish Options by Size</h2><table><thead><tr><th>Size</th><th>Available Materials</th><th>Available Closure Finishes</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>20/410</td><td>PP</td><td>Smooth, Ribbed, UV</td></tr><tr><td>24/410</td><td>PP, PP/PE, PET</td><td>Smooth, Ribbed, Aluminum, UV, Water Transfer Printing, Bamboo</td></tr><tr><td>28/410</td><td>PP, PP/PE, PET</td><td>Smooth, Ribbed, Aluminum, UV, Water Transfer Printing, Bamboo</td></tr><tr><td>28/412</td><td>PP, PP/PE</td><td>Smooth, Ribbed, Aluminum, UV, Water Transfer Printing, Bamboo</td></tr></tbody></table><p><!-- kb:v_product_full.standards closure finish options by size from product data --></p><h2>How to Measure Your Bottle Neck (If You Already Have Bottles)</h2><p>If you already have bottles and need matching disc top caps, measure these three dimensions with a caliper (not a ruler):</p><ol><li><strong>Neck outer diameter (OD):</strong> Measure across the crest of the bottle threads, not the neck wall below the threads. This is your “24” in 24/410.</li><li><strong>Thread pitch:</strong> Measure the vertical distance between two adjacent thread peaks. For 410 finish, this should be approximately 2.5–3.0mm.</li><li><strong>Thread turns:</strong> Count how many full rotations the thread makes around the neck. For 410 finish, this is typically 1.5 turns.</li></ol><p>If these measurements match a standard size within ±0.3mm, the corresponding cap size should fit. If they do not match any standard, your bottle has a non-standard neck finish and will require custom cap tooling — budget an additional $3,000–$5,000 for mold development.</p><h2>Ordering and Production Considerations</h2><p>When ordering disc top caps, size is not the only variable.
