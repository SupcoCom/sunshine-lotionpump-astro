disc top cap uses a spring-loaded disc mechanism: press one side of the disc, and it tilts open to expose a dispensing orifice; release, and it snaps back to seal. The disc is typically made of PP with a living hinge that must withstand <strong>thousands of open-close cycles without stress-whitening or cracking</strong>. At our Ningbo facility, disc top caps are molded from PP and tested for 5,000+ actuation cycles. The seal is achieved through a precision interference fit between the disc edge and the cap body — no separate gasket required.</p><h3>Flip Top Cap</h3><p>A flip top cap (also called a snap-top or hinged cap) uses an integrated hinge connecting the cap base to the lid. The lid snaps shut over the orifice with an audible click. The hinge is the failure point: if the polymer blend or mold temperature is off, the hinge will break after a few hundred flips. Our flip top caps use a PP/PE co-polymer blend specifically formulated for <strong>hinge durability exceeding 3,000 cycles</strong>. The orifice itself can be a simple round hole or a silicone valve for controlled dispensing.</p><h3>Screw Cap</h3><p>The screw cap is the simplest closure mechanically: a threaded cap that screws onto the bottle neck. No moving parts, no hinge, no spring. The seal is achieved through a liner (LDPE, EPE, or induction seal) compressed between the cap and the bottle land. Screw caps are the <strong>most leak-proof closure type</strong> when properly torqued, but they are also the least convenient for frequent dispensing — requiring two hands to open and close each time.</p><p><!-- kb:v_product_full disc cap and screw cap data --></p><h2>Head-to-Head Comparison: Disc Top vs Flip Top vs Screw Cap</h2><figure class="wp-block-image size-large diagram"><a href="https://sunshine-lotionpump.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2026/05/three-cap-comparison.png" target="_blank" rel="noopener"><img decoding="async" src="https://sunshine-lotionpump.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2026/05/three-cap-comparison.png" alt="Disc Top Cap vs Flip Top Cap vs Screw Cap — Mechanical Structure and Cycle Durability Comparison" style="max-width:100%;height:auto;display:block" /></a><figcaption>Fig 1: Disc Top Cap vs Flip Top Cap vs Screw Cap — Mechanical Structure and Cycle Durability Comparison</figcaption></figure><table><thead><tr><th>Feature</th><th>Disc Top Cap</th><th>Flip Top Cap</th><th>Screw Cap</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td><strong>One-Hand Operation</strong></td><td>Yes — press disc with thumb</td><td>Yes — flip lid with thumb</td><td>No — requires two hands</td></tr><tr><td><strong>Leak Resistance (Closed)</strong></td><td>Good — interference fit seal</td><td>Good — snap-fit with audible lock</td><td>Excellent — threaded compression with liner gasket</td></tr><tr><td><strong>Leak Resistance (In Bag)</strong></td><td>Moderate — accidental press can dispense</td><td>Moderate — lid can flip open under pressure</td><td>Excellent — cannot accidentally open</td></tr><tr><td><strong>Orifice Control</strong></td><td>Large orifice (3–5mm typical)</td><td>Small to medium orifice (1–4mm); Solution: specify impact-modified PP and inspect hinge cross-section under microscope during first-article approval.</li><li><strong>Leakage at disc seat:</strong> Caused by flash or sink marks on the sealing surface. Solution: verify mold maintenance schedule — cavity inserts should be polished every 100,000 shots.</li></ul><h3>Flip Top Cap Failures</h3><ul><li><strong>Hinge breakage:</strong> The #1 failure mode. Caused by wrong polymer blend or cold mold temperature during injection. Solution: specify PP/PE co-polymer with at least 300% elongation at break; verify mold temperature at 40–60°C during production.</li><li><strong>Lid won’t stay closed:</strong> Snap-fit undercut worn down after high-volume production. Solution: undercut depth should be 0.3–0.5mm minimum; measure on cavity samples every 50,000 shots.</li></ul><h3>Screw Cap Failures</h3><ul><li><strong>Thread stripping:</strong> Caused by cap threads not matching bottle neck threads (wrong finish specification). Solution: always test cap + bottle from the SAME production batch during approval.</li><li><strong>Liner delamination:</strong> Liner separates from cap, sticks to bottle land. Solution: specify induction-seal liner for liquid products; verify seal integrity with vacuum leak test.</li></ul><h2>Conclusion: A Decision Framework</h2><ol><li><strong>If your product is used in the shower or with one wet hand:</strong> choose Disc Top Cap — the press-to-dispense mechanism is unmatched for convenience.</li><li><strong>If your product needs maximum leak security for travel or long-term storage:</strong> choose Screw Cap with an induction seal liner.</li><li><strong>If your product requires precise, small-dose dispensing:</strong> choose Flip Top Cap with a silicone valve insert.</li><li><strong>For all closures:</strong> order samples with your actual bottle from the same factory — do not qualify caps and bottles from separate suppliers, as thread compatibility issues are the most common source of field failures.</li></ol><p>At Sunshine Plastic, we manufacture all three closure types in-house on dedicated production lines with <strong>MOQ of 10,000 units per type</strong>, <strong>standard lead time of 20 days</strong>, and <strong>free samples within 3–5 business days</strong>.
