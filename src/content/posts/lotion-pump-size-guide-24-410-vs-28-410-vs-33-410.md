These are not arbitrary — they encode the exact geometry of the pump-to-bottle interface.</p><p><strong>First number (neck diameter)</strong>: The inner diameter of the bottle neck opening, measured in millimeters. A 24/410 pump fits a 24mm neck; a 28/410 and 28/412 both fit a 28mm neck; a 33/410 fits a 33mm neck.</p><p><strong>Second number (thread specification)</strong>: Defined by the Society of Plastics Industry (SPI) / ASTM. The number indicates thread pitch and profile. “410” is the most common series for personal care closures — a continuous thread with a specific pitch count per inch. “412” uses a coarser pitch (fewer threads per inch) on the same 28mm diameter. When mixing 28/410 and 28/412 pumps and bottles, the neck diameter matches but the thread will not engage properly, causing leaks or pump failure.</p><table border="1" cellpadding="8" cellspacing="0" style="width:100%; border-collapse:collapse; margin:1.5em 0;"><caption style="font-weight:bold; margin-bottom:0.5em;">Table 1 — Pump Size Designation Reference Chart</caption><thead style="background:#f0f4f8;"><tr><th>Designation</th><th>Neck Diameter</th><th>Thread Type</th><th>Thread Pitch</th><th>Typical Bottle Volume</th><th>Typical Output/Stroke</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td><strong>24/410</strong></td><td>24 mm</td><td>410 continuous</td><td>Fine (standard)</td><td>30 ml – 150 ml</td><td>0.25 cc – 0.5 cc</td></tr><tr><td><strong>28/410</strong></td><td>28 mm</td><td>410 continuous</td><td>Fine (standard)</td><td>100 ml – 500 ml</td><td>1.0 cc – 2.0 cc</td></tr><tr><td><strong>28/412</strong></td><td>28 mm</td><td>412 continuous</td><td>Coarse</td><td>100 ml – 500 ml</td><td>1.0 cc – 2.0 cc</td></tr><tr><td><strong>33/410</strong></td><td>33 mm</td><td>410 continuous</td><td>Fine (standard)</td><td>300 ml – 1,000 ml</td><td>2.0 cc – 3.0 cc</td></tr></tbody></table><p><strong>Why does output per stroke matter?</strong> For a 500ml shampoo bottle used daily, a 2.0cc/stroke pump depletes the bottle in roughly 250 presses — approximately 4–6 weeks of average use. Ordering by diameter alone, ignoring thread type</strong><br /> “28mm pump” is not a complete specification. Always confirm 28/410 vs. 28/412. These look identical in photos but are not interchangeable. Request a physical sample fit-test against your production bottle before finalizing the order.</p><p><strong>2. Specifying the wrong dip tube length</strong><br /> Dip tube length must match bottle height. A tube that is 10mm too short leaves product at the bottom; 10mm too long creates turbulence and uneven dispensing. When ordering, provide the bottle height (from base to neck finish) and bottle shoulder depth to the pump supplier.</p><p><strong>3. Underestimating viscosity requirements</strong><br /> Standard 28/410 lotion pumps are rated for products up to approximately 5,000–8,000 cP. Products above this threshold — thick conditioners, face masks, silicone-heavy formulas — require a long-nozzle or extended-bore pump (e.g., WP-885). Specify formula viscosity in cP when requesting a quote.</p><p><strong>4. Neglecting to specify the lock mechanism for e-commerce SKUs</strong><br /> Pumps without a lock mechanism will discharge during courier transit, contaminating packaging and triggering returns. All SUNSHINE lock options (Up/Down, Left/Right, Screw) are included at no additional unit cost for orders above 10,000 units. Do not omit the lock spec.</p><p><strong>5. Confusing monthly capacity with available lead inventory</strong><br /> “500,000 units/month capacity” means production capacity, not finished goods on a shelf. If you need 500,000 units for a launch date, you must place the order 30–45 days before that date to allow for production scheduling, QC inspection, and sea freight (if applicable). Air freight options exist but increase per-unit logistics cost significantly.</p><h2>How to Evaluate a Lotion Pump Supplier: A Checklist That Uses Real Data</h2><p>When comparing lotion pump suppliers, reject any quote that cannot provide the following information in writing.
