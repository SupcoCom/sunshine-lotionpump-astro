Every actuation moves a fixed, geometrically defined volume of product — no more, no less — provided the formulation viscosity falls within the pump’s design range. Three design dimensions govern that fixed volume:</p><figure class="wp-block-image diagram"><a href="/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2026/05/lotion-pump-cross-section-1.png" target="_blank" rel="noopener"><br /><img decoding="async" src="/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2026/05/lotion-pump-cross-section-1.png" alt="Lotion pump internal cross-section diagram showing actuator, piston, chamber, spring, ball valve and dip tube with engineering callouts" /><br /></a><figcaption>Fig 2: Cross-section of a standard lotion pump. Output volume = πr² × L (bore radius² × stroke length). SUNSHINE dip tubes are cut to ±2mm of bottle depth as standard. Spring rated up to 50,000 cP formula viscosity.</figcaption></figure><h3>1. Chamber (Cylinder) Bore and Stroke Length</h3><p>The pump chamber is a hollow cylinder inside the actuator housing. Its bore diameter multiplied by its stroke length equals the swept volume per press. A 10mm bore with a 12.7mm stroke delivers approximately 1mL; doubling the bore to 14mm at the same stroke produces roughly 2mL. SUNSHINE lotion pumps for 24/410 and 28/410 neck finishes are engineered with precision-matched bore-stroke ratios to hold ±5% output tolerance batch-to-batch.</p><h3>2. Neck Finish (24/410, 28/410, 28/412)</h3><p>The neck finish number encodes both the thread diameter and pitch. A 24/410 pump fits a 24mm bottle neck with 10 threads per inch; a 28/410 uses a 28mm diameter at the same pitch; a 28/412 uses a 28mm diameter at 12 threads per inch. Critically, neck size constrains the maximum actuator housing diameter, which in turn caps the cylinder bore. This means:</p><ul><li><strong>24/410</strong> — typical output range: 0.8mL–2mL</li><li><strong>28/410</strong> — typical output range: 1.5mL–4mL</li><li><strong>28/412</strong> — typical output range: 1.5mL–4mL (higher-torque closure preferred for thick formulas)</li></ul><h3>3. Spring Rate and Return Speed</h3><p>The internal stainless-steel spring determines return speed after each press. A stiffer spring returns the piston faster, drawing product up through the dip tube more aggressively — important for thick emulsions that resist flow. Under-specing the spring for a high-viscosity formula produces partial dosing: the piston returns before the chamber is fully re-primed. SUNSHINE non-spill pumps use spring rates verified against formulas up to 50,000 cP (roughly the viscosity of thick body butter).</p><table><caption>Dosage Control Variables — Engineering Reference</caption><thead><tr><th>Variable</th><th>How It Affects Output</th><th>Designer’s Lever</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Chamber bore diameter</td><td>Larger bore → higher volume per stroke</td><td>Specified at mold design; fixed per SKU</td></tr><tr><td>Stroke length</td><td>Longer stroke → higher volume</td><td>Actuator geometry; adjustable by actuator cap height</td></tr><tr><td>Spring rate</td><td>Stiffer spring → reliable re-priming for thick formulas</td><td>Selected by material team per viscosity spec</td></tr><tr><td>Dip tube inner diameter</td><td>Wider tube → faster flow, shorter prime cycles</td><td>Specified per bottle depth and formula</td></tr><tr><td>Neck finish</td><td>Constrains max bore; determines lock mechanism type</td><td>Matched to bottle at packaging design stage</td></tr></tbody></table><h2>1mL Output: Precision Dosing for Concentrated Formulas</h2><p>A 1mL output pump dispenses approximately 20 drops per actuation.
