The following checklist separates suppliers who understand precision skincare dispensing from those who have rebranded a standard lotion pump as “treatment” for the skincare market.</p><p><strong>1. Can they provide dose accuracy data, not just dose claims?</strong> Ask for CV% (coefficient of variation) across 50 consecutive strokes from a filled production sample — not a claimed “0.25 ml per stroke” figure. A CV below 8% is acceptable; below 5% is good for premium skincare. If a supplier cannot provide this data, they have not measured it.</p><p><strong>2. Do they offer PP spring as a standard option, not a custom exception?</strong> Any treatment pump supplier serving the skincare market should have all-PP internal construction as a standard catalogue option, not a special-order request. Vitamin C and AHA formulas require it; a supplier who treats it as unusual doesn’t understand the category they’re serving.</p><p><strong>3. Can they confirm dip tube length tolerance against your specific bottle?</strong> The dip tube must reach within 3–5 mm of the bottle base. A supplier who provides a “standard length” without asking for your bottle dimensions will produce a pump that wastes 3–10% of your fill. Request dimensional drawings and specify the required dip tube insertion depth explicitly.</p><p><strong>4. Is Non-Spill certification tested per production batch, or only on prototype samples?</strong> Many suppliers test Non-Spill on a pre-production prototype and then ship production units without re-testing. SUNSHINE tests Non-Spill on every production batch and includes the test certificate with each shipment. Confirm this in writing on the purchase order.</p><p><strong>5. Can they provide material compatibility documentation for your specific formula pH?</strong> For any formula below pH 4.0, a credible supplier should be able to confirm spring material (SS304 vs PP) suitability and offer immersion test data at your target pH. “It should be fine” is not an acceptable answer for a premium skincare formula.</p><p><strong>6. What is their full-drawdown residual on a 30 ml bottle?</strong> Ask for a full-drawdown test result: fill a 30 ml bottle, actuate until the pump fails, weigh residual formula. Acceptable: below 1 ml (3.3%). Common failure: 2–4 ml residual (7–13%) — a significant dose loss that consumers will notice when their serum runs out two weeks earlier than expected.</p><h2>Common Mistakes When Specifying a Treatment Pump</h2><p><strong>1. Specifying neck size without specifying output volume</strong><br /> “24/410 treatment pump” is insufficient. The same 24/410 neck can hold a 0.08 ml pump (for concentrated eye serum) or a 0.30 ml pump (for a richer moisturizer). State the target dose in ml/stroke on every purchase order and sample request — not as a courtesy, as a technical requirement.</p><p><strong>2. Using a stainless spring on a vitamin C formula below pH 3.5</strong><br /> The result is brown rust particles in a clear serum within 6–9 months of shelf life. This creates product recalls and brand damage. Specify PP spring on any formula below pH 4.0 as a standard precaution. The PP spring costs no more and adds no lead time.</p><p><strong>3. Not testing dip tube length against the actual bottle</strong><br /> Treatment pump bottles are taller and slimmer than lotion pump bottles. A standard dip tube from a lotion pump fitted to a serum bottle will leave 2–4 ml of formula at the bottle bottom unreachable. Request a full-drawdown test (fill to 95%, actuate until empty, weigh residual) before approving the pump for production. Residual should be below 3% of fill volume — below 1 ml in a 30 ml bottle.</p><p><strong>4. Ordering airless without a formula need</strong><br /> Airless pumps cost 40–80% more than standard treatment pumps. If your formula is stable in a standard pump with adequate antioxidant buffer (verified by 12-month ICH Q1A stability), the premium is wasted. Run stability testing with both configurations before committing to airless.</p><p><strong>5. Approving crimp format before confirming filling line capability</strong><br /> Crimp closures require a specialized crimper at the filling line. If your filler doesn’t have one, you cannot use the crimp format regardless of what’s been ordered.
